I am in my final year at Shanghai Pinghe School. I dance, I have played floorball for the Pinghe Dragons since first grade, and for the past four years I have taught programming to beginners at a volunteer organization in Shanghai.

The projects here were not planned as a set. A shot model built on NHL data, a study of how language models react to small changes in a prompt, a measurement problem in a mathematics essay. Underneath them is the same short list of questions about noise, measurement, and what a system's behaviour is actually made of, and each one ends up asking what its measurement can and cannot resolve.

I do not have a narrow specialism yet and would rather not claim one. What I do think is that the tools for doing this kind of work are changing quickly enough to change which questions are worth attempting at all, and I would like to be working where that matters.

02NHL shot modelling · 956,147 attempts

A shot model, and the ceiling it hit

Five models, scored on 200,820 held-out shots, landing within half a percentage point of each other. That is the finding rather than the disappointment: all five are trained on the same features, and goalkeeper position, defender angle and screening are not among them. The limit is the data, not the architecture.
